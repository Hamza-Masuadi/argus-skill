# Stage 1: the brief

**If `.argus/run.md` exists, read it first**, and again after any compaction or when resuming.

Produces: the answers in `DESIGN.md`, the brief in ten lines, two or three headlines with subheads, the proof given and three objections, written to `run.md`.

## The rules, most important first

- **Write the brief before any design choice.** Every later choice (the header, the sections, the pictures, the motion) is checked against these lines, so they must exist first.
- **The brief has five parts**: what the site is for; who arrives and from where (the situation, not a demographic); the one action; why they would choose this over the alternative (the difference); what they should be sure of after one screen (the belief). Take them from the brief, `DESIGN.md` and the answers.
- **Headlines come from the difference or the belief, never from the layout.** A headline taken from the picture describes the picture, not the business. Write two or three now, short (often under eight words), in words the visitor would use; every direction takes its headline from this list.
- **Each headline has a subhead, and the pair passes the offer test**: a stranger can say what this is, for whom or where, and the outcome (`convert.md`, "The offer in one breath"). An offer nobody understands makes every later stage decorate confusion.
- **The owner's taste is recorded here and binds every direction later**: what they admire, what they said would be wrong for them, how the business behaves.
- **Proof is asked for, never written** (`convert.md`, "Proof"); **three objections are named now**, from the answers or inferred from the kind of business.

## The competitors' first screens (optional)

When the session has a web search or browser tool and the competitors are named or easy to find, look at the two or three nearest for a few minutes: first screens only, no more than three sites. Then write:

```
They all say:   the claim every one of them leads with
They all look:  the ground, the picture and the layout they share
This page:      what it will do differently, from the difference
```

The third line names something this business does or has, not a style. Nothing is copied: what they all share is what this page does not lead with. Record the three lines in `DESIGN.md` under "This page".

## The customers' words (Persuade pages)

Separately, where the tool allows, read about twenty reviews of the business or of its nearest competitors and note the phrases customers use for the problem and the outcome. They feed the headlines, subheads and the answers to objections. They are phrasings, not proof: never quote a competitor's review as if it were about this business. If nothing can be read, or the business is invented, use the owner's own phrases and say so in the report.

## What to read

- **Whole**: `ask.md`, before asking; `convert.md` on a Persuade page, before the headlines (it is used again at the first screen and the build, from your notes).
- **Not yet**: the craft references; nothing is queried before the kit exists.

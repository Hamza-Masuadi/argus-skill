# Tells

Last revised: 2026-11. These drift as models change.

A tell is a choice that appears because it is the default, and readers have learned to recognise it. None of these is forbidden. Each is fine when the brief or the existing system asks for it. The question to ask of every one found: was this chosen, or did it just arrive?

After replacing a tell, check the replacement is not simply the next most common default. This is the known weakness of any list like this one: avoiding the listed looks tends to produce the next most common look, which then becomes the new cliché. The list cannot fix that on its own. What does is a direction taken from the specific subject, a reference from the user, and the "Tried" log in `DESIGN.md`.

If the user asked for something on this list, build it well and do not flag it.

**Chosen richness is not a tell.** A gradient, glow, blur, shadow or texture that comes from the direction (light from a lamp, the sky at a time of day, depth between layers, the grain of the kit's own surface) is craft, and depth and light are wanted (`create.md` step 6, `imagery.md`). So is motion with a purpose from the subject: a route travelled, a form filling, a drawing completing. The tells below are the unchosen defaults: the same effect laid on everything, with no source in the subject.

For new Persuade work, skim this file before writing headlines and directions, since several tells (the headline, the header, the default looks) are decided there; when looking at the roughs and when checking, re-open only "Whole default looks".

## Whole default looks

Most tells do not arrive alone. They arrive as a whole look: a set of three or four features that come together because each makes the next one feel natural. Each look below is named with its features. Landing in one without being asked means the direction step was skipped, or the idea was not carried into the page.

**Three shared features send it back to the idea.** A rough or a page that shares three features with one look goes back to its idea (`create.md` step 2), not to a different coat of paint. Changing the accent, swapping the face or moving the headline leaves the same look in other clothes. Go back to the kit item the direction is built on and let it set the ground, the type and the composition. One or two shared features are not this rule: judge them one by one in the tables below.

**Minimal is one way to be specific, not the goal.** Removing things is not the cure for a default look; the studio portfolio below is what that cure produces. A plain page passes when the one thing on each screen could only belong to this business (the swap test, `create.md` step 7).

### The warm default
- A cream, beige, yellow, kraft-brown or amber ground, often flooding the whole page.
- A serif display face, often with a terracotta or amber accent.
- Soft rounded cards and gentle shadows around everything.
- Reached for because the brief said "warm", "friendly" or "handmade".

Instead: two tinted neutrals and one accent (`ingredients.md`). Find warmth in a deep green, a brick red, an ink blue on warm paper, or in type, shape and wording; take the hue from a named thing in the subject's world.

### The paper edition
- A serif headline with one word in italic and in the accent colour.
- Small monospace labels above things: section numbers, categories, dates.
- An off-white ground.
- Hairline rules between everything, as in a newspaper.

Instead: hierarchy by size and space; emphasis by wording, the headline in one colour; monospace only for actual code or data; a ground taken from the subject.

### The paper artefact on off-white
- A pale off-white, pale grey or grey-green ground.
- The headline on the left.
- A "paper document" from the subject (a page from a notebook, a form, a receipt, a ticket) as a card on the right.
- Skeuomorphic paper as the default way to make a subject feel real: ruled lines, stacked sheets, stamps, handwriting faces.

Each instance looks defensible; across unrelated subjects it is the same picture. Instead: show the kit as itself: a photograph of the real thing, the product working, or the page taking the thing's form at full width. Paper only where paper is what the customer actually handles, and then not as a card beside the headline. In a set of directions, at most one uses a pale neutral ground, at most one stages its idea as a paper object, and the first-screen compositions differ (`create.md` step 2). No handwriting or script face for headlines unless the user supplied it.

### The dark stage
- A dark ground.
- A very large condensed all-capitals headline.
- One warm accent: orange, amber or red.
- Reached for because it looks dramatic.

Instead: choose light or dark from where and when the thing is used. Let the kit set the ground (a chalk slate, pattern paper, the white of a boarding card) and take the display face from the subject's own lettering (a painted sign over a counter, a terminal's stencilled signs, a record label's type). In a set of directions, at most one is dark and at most one uses a condensed all-capitals display face.

### The launch page
- A black or near-black ground.
- A purple or blue glow, or a purple-to-blue gradient, behind the headline.
- A product screenshot tilted, floating or set in perspective.
- A centred stack: a small badge, the headline, a subhead, two buttons.

Instead: the product shown flat and working, at a size where it can be read, in a real state with this business's data (`imagery.md`). Light or dark from where it is used; a gradient only where it means something (daylight, distance, a lamp).

### The dashboard default
- A row of four figure cards, each with a number, a change and a sparkline.
- A large line or area chart under them.
- A table under that.
- Every block in the same rounded card.

Instead: lead with the question this person arrives with and the thing they act on (`product.md`). Show a figure only where someone decides something from it, and size blocks by importance, not into an even row.

### The dark developer tool
- A near-black ground.
- Monospace for most of the text, not only the code.
- A green or cyan accent.
- Terminal blocks, often typing themselves, as the hero.

Instead: monospace only for the real commands and output; show what the tool really does on a real example; a dark ground only with a reason from how it is used (it sits beside a dark editor all day), never because the audience is developers.

### The studio portfolio
The overcorrection when told not to be generic.
- An off-white ground.
- One giant headline filling the width.
- Numbered monospace labels: 01, 02, 03.
- A clock, a city name or coordinates in the header.

Instead: one specific thing from this business on the first screen (its rule, its price, its place, its hours, a number only it has), and a look taken from its kit. Looking unlike a template is not the same as looking like this business.

### Other whole-page habits

| Tell | Instead |
|---|---|
| Near-black background with a neon or acid accent and a glow on everything | Choose dark only when the place of use calls for it; light and glow only where the direction names their source |
| A purple-to-blue or purple-to-pink gradient on every surface, chosen by nobody | Gradients that mean something (daylight, distance, a lamp, depth), in hues from the subject; one accent for actions |
| Thick borders and hard offset shadows on everything | Borders and shadows only where they separate something |
| Drawn illustration throughout, the page made as a crafted object, for a subject that has real things to photograph or a product to show | Photographs of the kit, or the product's real interface (`imagery.md`); drawing only for diagrams, maps, or when asked. In a set of directions, one is in the plain register (`create.md` step 2) |

## Layout

| Tell | Instead |
|---|---|
| Centred hero: badge, headline, subhead, two buttons | Lead with the product, a real image or the content itself; one primary action |
| Three equal cards, each icon + heading + two lines | Fewer, unequal items; or a list, a comparison, a real screenshot |
| Small all-caps label above every heading | Let the heading stand alone. (A label that is real information, such as a car's make above its model, or one of a set of real markings, such as sizes along a tape or lane numbers on a deck, is not this tell.) |
| Row of big statistics with invented numbers | Real figures with sources, or leave it out |
| Every section in a rounded card | Space and alignment; boxes only for things that are objects |
| Bordered cards, boxes and rules around every group | Separate with space and alignment first; a box only around a distinct object (`craft.md`) |
| Buttons in several sizes, shapes and colours on one page | One button style and size for the action; `check.js` measures it |
| A photograph shrunk into a card with a heading under it | The photograph edge to edge or filling its column (`photos.md`) |
| A gimmick per section: answers behind drawn objects to click, a game to reach a price | The information as readable text; a device only where it is easier than a list (`create.md` step 3) |
| `01 / 02 / 03` numbering on content that is not a sequence | Number only real steps |
| Logo strip of invented customers; invented testimonials | Real ones, or none |

## Detail

| Tell | Instead |
|---|---|
| Emoji used as icons | One icon set, used consistently, or no icons |
| Gradient-filled headline text; one coloured word in a headline | Solid text; emphasis by wording |
| Glass panels for no reason: frosted cards over a blurred blob | Solid surfaces; blur only where one layer really sits in front of another, or where the platform uses it |
| A glow behind every card or button | Light from the one source the direction names, or none |
| Coloured left border on callout cards | Background tint or an icon |
| Pill-shaped everything | One radius language chosen in the direction |
| Arrow after every link | Plain links |
| Pulsing "live" dot with nothing live behind it | Remove it |
| The first sans-serif that comes to mind, used for everything | A family chosen for the subject, or the platform's system font used deliberately. One family for everything is right in the plain register when it is chosen for the subject and set with care: two or three weights, tight headline tracking |
| Three or more families, or four or more weights | At most two families (measured by `check.js`) and three weights |

## Motion

Motion with a purpose from the subject is wanted (`craft.md`). These are motion with no subject:

| Tell | Instead |
|---|---|
| Everything fades up identically on scroll: same distance, same timing, every block | Reveals that differ by what each section is (a drawing completes, a list deals in), staggered, from the idea |
| Parallax for its own sake: background layers drifting behind content that has no depth | Layers that part because the subject has layers; otherwise none |
| A floating or morphing blob, orb or gradient mesh | The live element, drawn from the subject's own gesture |
| A typewriter headline | The headline arrives whole, with the rest of the first screen |
| Numbers that count up but mean nothing (invented totals, "10,000+") | Count only real figures, to their real value, or show them still |

## Copy

| Tell | Instead |
|---|---|
| "Elevate", "seamless", "unlock", "supercharge", "effortless", "reimagine" | Say what the product does, with a verb and an object |
| Three-part rhythm in every line ("Fast. Simple. Secure.") | One specific claim |
| "It's not just X, it's Y" | State Y |
| Headlines that would fit any company | Name the thing, the user or the result |
| "John Doe", "Acme Inc.", round numbers like 10,000 | Plausible, specific stand-ins, marked as assumed |

# Ask

For new work whose look is still open. The answers are what make this design the owner's instead of the one any model would produce. So: **one message, then wait; then the choosing page, then wait; then build.**

## When to ask

**If a person can answer, always send the one question message before designing, and wait for the reply.** Assume a person can answer in any interactive session, unless the request says otherwise or the harness clearly has no way to ask. Use the harness's question tool when there is one (AskUserQuestion, for example), so the owner taps choices; otherwise ask in plain text. A detailed brief is not permission to skip the owner.

**Skip it only when the owner's request says so in words** ("don't ask", "no questions", "just build", "decide yourself") **or the run is unattended.** Then every question takes its recommended answer, and `run.md` lists each one as "assumed". "It is a benchmark", "the brief is complete", "nobody to answer" decided by you, or the size of the build are not reasons.

## The message

- **Start with one or two lines of what you took from the brief** ("From your brief: a defence-technology group with four divisions; investors and engineers are the visitors; a contact form is the action"), so the owner can correct it.
- **Then ask the decisions that are the owner's taste and cannot be read from a brief**: about five or six questions, each with choices and your recommended answer marked, and room for their own words.
- A question the brief answered word for word is not asked again. Taste, pictures and motion are always confirmed, briefly, with the recommended answer marked.
- Recommended answers are specific to this business. Options are a short label and one plain sentence. Never ask for fonts, hex values or a named style ("minimal or brutalist?"): that is your job.
- There is no second round. Further questions wait until there is something to look at.

## The questions

**1. What do you like, and what do you dislike?** Sites or qualities they admire or cannot stand; light or dark; calm or bold. Offer concrete pairs from this business ("calm and plain, like a good annual report" / "bold and graphic, like a launch poster"). The answer becomes `--taste`, `--tone` and `--no` (`directions.md`).

**2. What would be wrong for you, even if it looked good?** Offer two or three (too corporate for a one-person trade, stock smiles where they have real photos, text beside a photo like every other site). On a Persuade page, add the likeliest reasons a visitor would not act (a price surprise, time, lock-in, "will it work for me?") as options to confirm (`convert.md`, "Objections").

**3. Pictures: your own, stock, generated, or none?** Offer "generated" only when an image-generation tool is in this session (check the tool list; never assume one): one light, their colours, noted once in the footer, listed to replace. Recommend their own when they have them, else stock photographs Argus finds; generated only when the owner or the brief asks for it (`pictures.md`).

**4. How much should move?** **Still**: nothing moves but controls answering. **Moment** (recommended for business pages): the first screen arrives, sections reveal as they enter, every control responds, and one choreographed moment made for the subject. **Showpiece** (recommended for a portfolio, studio, launch, fashion, culture or brand site): scenes, page transitions, smooth scroll (`showpiece.md`).

**5. What can I work from?** A logo, brand colours, screenshots of the product, a site they admire. On a Persuade page, ask for proof in the same question: "Your rating and number of reviews; real reviews word for word, with first name and date; years trading; accreditations; guarantees; press; staff who can be named." Recommend "I'll paste what I have"; a skipped answer means none was given, never that some may be written (`convert.md`, "Proof").

**6. Only what the brief truly left open**: who it is for and what has just happened to them; why they would choose this business over the nearest alternative; what happens when they say yes. If the folder is empty and the brief does not say how to build, "Plain HTML or a framework?" (plain HTML recommended); for a mobile app of unknown platform, "iPhone, Android or both?".

## After the reply

- Restate what you took from the answers in two or three lines, so a misunderstanding is caught early.
- A reference is the strongest input there is: study its palette, type, density, layout idea and tone, and say which you will carry over. Brand colours and a logo they have are kept, not reinvented.
- Record the answers in `DESIGN.md` straight away, under "Product" and "This page" (`design-md.md`, "Shape"), and set `Asked:` in `run.md`.
- The choice between designs comes after the owner has looked: build the directions, show the choosing page, then ask which, and wait (`directions.md`).

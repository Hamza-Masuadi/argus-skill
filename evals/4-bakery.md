# Ember & Rye

Build the website for Ember & Rye, a neighbourhood sourdough bakery. You have complete creative freedom: develop the identity, typography, colour, layout, motion and copy yourself. Do not ask me to choose anything. The result should feel like a real, loved local bakery with an exceptionally good website, not a template.

## The business
- Ember & Rye, a wood-fired sourdough bakery on Calder Lane, Bristol, open since 2017. Founder and head baker: Nell Okafor. Six staff. Everything is baked overnight and sold the same morning; when it's gone, it's gone.
- Customers: locals who queue on Saturday, parents ordering for the weekend, a few cafes. Most arrive from Instagram, a neighbour, or a search like "sourdough Bristol".
- Why here: 36-hour fermented loaves from three flours milled in Somerset; you can order by 6pm and collect from 7:30 the next morning without queuing.
- Main action: order for collection tomorrow (choose loaves and pastries, choose a collection time, pay at the counter). Second: the weekly bread subscription (one loaf every Saturday, £18 a month, pause any time).
- Open Wed–Sun 7:30–2:00, or until sold out.

## The bake (invent the rest in the same spirit; at least 10 items)
- Country sourdough £4.80; Seeded rye £5.20; Malted wheat tin £4.60; Olive and rosemary £5.60 (Fri–Sun); Cardamom bun £3.20; Morning bun £3.40; Almond croissant £3.80; Cheese and marmite swirl £3.60.
Each item needs a name, a one-line description, a price, the days it is baked, and allergens.

## Proof
- Google 4.9 from 1,140 reviews. About 900 loaves a week.
- Reviews, word for word: "Ordered at 5pm, picked up at 8, walked past a forty-person queue." (Dan, October 2026); "The rye lasts us all week and is still good on Friday." (Shreya, September 2026); "My kids now refuse supermarket bread. Thanks for that." (Olu, August 2026).
- What stops people: it sells out; the queue; "is it worth £5 for a loaf?"; not knowing what to choose.

## Page
One page plus a working order flow (basket, collection time, confirmation; no payment, do not pretend one was taken). It should include: an opening that makes you want the bread, today's bake with what is still available, how ordering for tomorrow works, the subscription, the bakers and the oven, where and when, and a strong ending. Mobile matters: most visitors are on a phone on Friday evening.

## Photographs
Use real stock photographs of bread, pastry, hands and ovens, chosen as one consistent, sharp, well-lit set. The business is invented: say so once in the footer.

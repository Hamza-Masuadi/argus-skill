# Ask

For new work with an open brief. The user's answers are what make this design theirs instead of the one any model would produce, so a short round of questions is worth more than any rule in this skill. Asked badly it is also the most annoying thing a design tool does. Keep it to one round.

## How to ask

- Use the environment's question tool if there is one, so the user can answer by clicking. Otherwise ask in one short message.
- Four questions about the product at most, plus the two about the work itself (5 and 6 below) for a new page. Drop any the brief already answers; if it answers them all, ask nothing.
- Every question can be skipped, and every question comes with your recommended answer already chosen, so the user can accept the lot in one click. People skip question forms more often than they fill them in; a form that works when submitted unchanged still gets you a confirmed starting point.
- Offer choices where that helps, always with room for their own words.
- Ask about the product, the people and the feeling. Never ask for fonts, hex values or a named style ("minimal or brutalist?"): that is your job, and naming a style in a question gets it chosen. The one question about form is the first screen (5 below), and its options are written for this subject, never as style labels.
- One round, then build. Further questions wait until there is something to look at.
- If there is nobody to ask, write down the assumption you are making for each question and carry on.

## The questions

Pick the ones the brief leaves open, in this order of value.

**1. What do you have that shows what you want?**
"Is there a site, app or screenshot you'd like this to feel like, or a logo, brand colours or photos to work from?" A reference is the strongest input there is. If they share one, study its palette, type, density, layout idea and tone, and tell them which of those you will carry over.

**2. Who is this for, and what should they do here?**
"Who is the main person using this, and what's the one thing you want them to do?" Skip if the brief says. The answer sets the surface, the tone and the primary action.

**3. How should it feel?**
Offer three or four pairs and ask where it sits, for example:
- calm or energetic
- playful or serious
- spacious or dense
- familiar or surprising
- warm or technical

These are about the product's character, not its styling. Choose pairs that are genuinely open for this product; do not ask "serious or playful" about a tax tool for accountants.

**4. What must be on it, and what must it avoid?**
"Anything it has to include (prices, real customers, specific sections) or anything you don't want?" This is where real content comes from, and where you learn what they have already rejected.

**5. What should the first screen be built around?** (new pages only)
Do not offer a fixed menu. Think about this subject first and propose **three approaches that would each suit it**, described in plain words as what the visitor would see, with the one you recommend chosen. They should be different kinds of first screen, drawn from the list of moves in `create.md` step 3, for example for a sleeper-train service:

- "A large drawn map of the routes north, with the search on top of it" (a picture)
- "The journey search itself, working, with a live result" (the product, live)
- "One very large line of text and almost nothing else" (scale)

Each option is one line, specific to this product, and says what is big. Always leave room for their own words ("or describe your own"). If photographs were supplied, one option uses the best of them. The answer fixes the kind of first screen; the directions in question 6 then differ in idea, structure and look within it. If they skip it, the directions may each use a different approach.

**6. How many directions do you want to see before I build?** (new pages only)
Offer **1, 3 or 5, with 3 chosen**. Five is the most. Each direction is a different idea for the whole design, shown as its first screen, side by side on one page, for them to pick or mix (`create.md` step 4). One means you choose for them and go straight to building. Say what it costs in plain terms: more directions take longer.

For a mobile app where the platform is unknown, ask "iPhone, Android or both?" in place of one of the above.

## After the answers

- Restate what you took from them in two or three lines as you start, so a misunderstanding is caught early. Do not wait for a reply.
- Record them under "Decisions" in `DESIGN.md` when one exists, so they are not asked again.
- The choice between designs is not a question to ask in words. Build the number of directions they asked for, show them side by side (step 4 of `create.md`) and let them pick by looking.

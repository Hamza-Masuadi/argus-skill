# Stage 2: the kit and the ideas

**If `.argus/run.md` exists, read it first**, and again after any compaction or when resuming.

Produces: the kit and tone, the survivors with their kinds and fingerprints, the picture decision and the photo batch, written to `run.md`.

## The rules, most important first

- **Write the kit before listing a single idea.** Pages that feel as if they understand the business show what the customer handles, does or gets, not a mood or a metaphor from nearby; ideas listed first come from habit.
- **The kit has five kinds of thing**, eight to ten items in all: objects handled; instruments and documents; gestures and moments; systems of marking; phrases people really say there. Things from the building or the street rank last.
- **An idea says what leads the first screen and why it convinces this visitor**: "the finished kitchen, photographed large, with the price per metre beside the action". A mechanism the visitor sees happen is one kind of idea, suited to the expressive direction, not required of the others.
- **What leads answers the visitor's question fastest**: the product or result shown clearly, the person they will meet, the price, the proof.
- **The owner's taste bounds every candidate** (`run.md`, Taste).

## The cut

- Cross out anything under "Tried" in `DESIGN.md`, and what the owner's answers rule out.
- Cross out a candidate a stranger would not understand in five seconds, or one whose cleverness hides what the business sells.
- Ask each survivor: without the logo, could you tell what kind of business this is from the first screen? Rework or drop one that fails.
- Two survivors whose fingerprints share their first two lines are one idea in different clothes: replace one.

## One idea, one or two moments

The pages owners like have **one idea that runs through them** ("the sea picks the route"), carried by **one or two moments**: places where the page gets rich, such as a visual made for this business, an interaction, a strong photograph or a drawing. Every other section is well set and serves its content: a good ruled list, a clear price table, a calm FAQ are right answers. Pages that give every section its own costume from the trade lose the product among the props. Plain is not the same as pale or empty: a section's room is earned by the strong thing in it.

For software, the product shown clearly and working is the moment; props from the trade are rarely right.

## The fingerprint

Each survivor gets four short lines, written to `run.md`:

```
Leads with:   what the first screen is built around, naming its kit item
Order:        the sections, in sequence
Headings:     where they sit and how big (left rail, above, inline, oversized, none)
Rhythm:       how density changes down the page (for example: loud, dense, quiet, dense, loud)
```

If it reads "hero, three features, alternating blocks, pricing, call to action", choose again: that structure should be chosen, never arrived at. Other structures to bend: the product is the page, one claim, the catalogue, the sequence, the comparison, the index, the configurator, the letter, the evidence, the place.

## The habit check

Some forms turn up by habit: the side-by-side header, sticky steps or a scroll timeline, stacking cards, a bento grid, a count-up stat row, a marquee, a card grid of three. Each is fine when the content has that shape; the fault is reaching for it first.

For each main section write the content's shape (a sequence? a comparison? one object? a place? a list that changes daily?) and pick the plainest form that fits it: a sequence as numbered steps, a comparison as a table, a list as a list. The cure for habit is choosing by shape, not dressing the section in a metaphor.

## The three kinds

The default set is one of each: **minimal** (few elements and one accent, the room filled by one strong thing), **picture-led** (sharp, true photographs or the product carry the page) and **expressive** (a bolder idea, an interaction or a committed colour, still clear in five seconds). None of them means a pale page by default. Swap a kind when the brief rules it out and say so. **Every direction is a real contender**, finished to the same standard.

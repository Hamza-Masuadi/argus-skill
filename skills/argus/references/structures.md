# Page structures

Most generated pages share one skeleton: a centred or split hero, a row of features, a few alternating blocks, pricing, a closing call to action. Changing the colours and fonts on that skeleton gives a page that looks different and feels the same. Real variety is structural: what the first screen is built around, what order the argument runs in, where headings sit, and how density rises and falls.

Use this file in `create.md` step 2, when listing candidate ideas, and in step 4, when building rough alternatives. It is a catalogue to choose from and bend, not a set of templates.

## Structures

Each is defined by what leads and how the page then unfolds.

| Structure | The first screen is | Then | Suits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The product is the page** | A working piece of the product, large; the headline is its caption | The same piece taken further: other states, other cases, the rest of the product | Software whose value shows in use |
| **One claim** | A single sentence at great size, almost nothing else | Each section answers the next objection to that sentence, in order | A clear, contestable promise |
| **The catalogue** | The things themselves: items, listings, a grid, with a way to narrow them | Detail on demand; the story comes after the stock | Shops, portfolios, directories, showrooms |
| **The artefact** | The page takes the form of a real object from the subject's world: a ticket, a label, a ledger, a menu | Its parts are the sections; you read it as you would the object | Subjects with a strong physical or documentary form |
| **The sequence** | The first step of something that happens in order | Each screen is the next step or moment, with time or progress visible | Processes, journeys, a day with the product |
| **The comparison** | Two states side by side: before and after, with and without, them and us | The same comparison taken through each area that matters | Products that replace an existing way of doing something |
| **The index** | A list of contents on one side and the selected item on the other | Choosing changes the main view; little scrolling | Several distinct offerings of equal weight; reference material |
| **The configurator** | A few choices the visitor makes, and a result that updates | The result becomes the offer; detail supports the choices | Plans, bundles, estimates, anything priced by choice |
| **The letter** | Prose, addressed to the reader, in a single readable column | It stays prose, with the occasional figure or pull-out | Founders' pages, manifestos, services sold on trust |
| **The evidence** | One piece of proof: a result, a number with its source, a case | The method behind it, more cases, then the offer | Subjects where the reader arrives sceptical |
| **The question** | The question the visitor arrived with, stated in their words | Answers, most asked first, each leading to the offer | Unfamiliar categories; support-led products |
| **The place** | Where it is and when: a map, hours, what is on now | What you will find there, how to get in, how to book | Venues, events, local businesses |

## The fingerprint

Before building, write the page's fingerprint in four short lines:

```
Leads with:   what the first screen is built around
Order:        the sections, in sequence
Headings:     where they sit and how big (left rail, above, inline, oversized, none)
Rhythm:       how density changes down the page (for example: loud, dense, quiet, dense, loud)
```

Then check it:

- **Against the default.** If it reads "hero, three features, alternating blocks, pricing, call to action", choose again. That structure is correct for some pages, but it should be chosen, never arrived at.
- **Against the alternatives.** In `create.md` step 4, the rough alternatives must differ in at least the first two lines of their fingerprints. Three palettes on one structure are one alternative.
- **Against the project.** Compare with the fingerprints under "Tried" in `DESIGN.md`. A new page for the same product should not repeat the last one's structure unless they are meant as a set.

## Where the action goes

Whatever the structure, the visitor can act from the first screen, and again at the point where they have been convinced.

- Structures that build toward a conclusion (the sequence, one claim, the letter, the evidence) carry the action at the start and repeat it at the end. Repeating the action is not repeating content.
- Structures that are themselves the action (the catalogue, the configurator, the index) keep it attached to each item or to the result.
- The action looks and reads the same wherever it appears.

## Bending a structure

- Structures combine: a catalogue whose first item is shown as an artefact; a sequence that ends in a configurator.
- The first screen's structure need not hold all the way down, but the page should have one clear organising idea, not a different one per section.
- Every structure still has to make the whole argument (`create.md` step 3). A structure changes the order and form of the answers, not which questions get answered.
- On phones, structures built on two sides (comparison, index) need a deliberate single-column form: stacked with clear labels, or a toggle. Decide it when choosing the structure.

## Headings and rhythm

Two cheap sources of variety that are usually left at their defaults:

- **Heading placement.** Centred above the content is one option of several. Headings can sit in a left column beside their content, run inline as the first words of a paragraph, be very large and few, or be absent where the content names itself.
- **Rhythm.** A page where every section has the same height, padding and density reads as a list. Let one section be much denser than its neighbours, one much emptier, and let the important one take more room. A nearly empty section still has its device: one figure, one line, one image given the whole space.

# Landfall

Build the marketing website for Landfall, shipment-tracking software for small importers. You have complete creative freedom: develop the identity, typography, colour, layout, motion and copy yourself. Do not ask me to choose anything. It should feel like a real, confident small software company with an exceptionally good website, not a SaaS template.

## The product
- Landfall shows a small importer where every container is, when it will really arrive, and what it will cost to land, in one place. It reads the carrier, port and customs updates the importer already gets by email and portal, and turns them into one date per shipment that it keeps correcting. Made in Rotterdam and Felixstowe since 2022 by a team of fourteen; founder Annelies de Vries spent nine years as a freight forwarder.
- Customers: owner-run importers and small brands bringing in 5 to 200 containers a year (furniture, bikes, homeware, food), who today track shipments in a spreadsheet, carrier websites and a forwarder's emails. The reader is the owner or operations lead, not technical, worried about stock-outs and surprise port charges.
- Why Landfall: one honest arrival date per shipment with a stated confidence and the reason it moved; a warning before demurrage and storage charges start, with the daily cost; landed cost per unit before the goods arrive; it works with the forwarder you already have.
- Main action: start a free trial (work email, no card, first three shipments free). Second: see a sample shipment.
- Pricing: £9 per container tracked, nothing monthly, no contract.

## Proof
- 1,150 importers; 86,000 containers tracked; arrival dates within one day of actual for 91% of shipments at the time of vessel departure (2026, customers' own shipments); Trustpilot 4.7 from 540 reviews.
- Reviews, word for word: "It told me the box would miss the feeder four days before my forwarder did." (Rob, furniture importer, Leeds); "The storage warning has paid for the year twice over." (Sunita, homeware brand, Leicester); "I stopped opening six carrier websites every morning." (Pieter, bike importer, Utrecht).
- What stops people: "my forwarder already tells me"; setting it up; trusting a date from software; whether it covers their carriers and ports.

## Page
One page. It should include: an opening that makes the promise plain, one shipment followed from factory gate to warehouse with its date moving and why, the charges warning, landed cost, what it connects to, pricing with a simple calculator, the objections answered, and a strong ending. The sample shipment should be real enough to explore.

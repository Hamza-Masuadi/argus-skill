# Content that converts

For Persuade pages. A page converts when a stranger understands the offer at once, believes it, finds their hesitation answered, and knows what to do next. The design carries that argument; it cannot stand in for it.

## The offer in one breath

The headline and the subhead together say three things: what this is, for whom or where, and the outcome the visitor gets. A visitor gives a new page a few seconds, and one who cannot say what it is leaves.

- **Clarity beats cleverness.** A belief line may be the headline only if the subhead names the category and the place or audience in its first five words ("Piano tuning across Bristol, ..."). Otherwise the headline names the thing: a line the visitor has to decode costs the seconds the page has.
- **The "only we" test.** Could a rival put their name on the claim unchanged? Add the specific: the price, the place, the hour, the rule or the number only they have. This is the swap test (`first-screen.md`, the bar) applied to each sentence.

## Words

- **Benefit before feature, concrete before abstract.** Numbers, times, prices and names beat adjectives: "£89 fixed, parts included" beats "transparent pricing", because the visitor can check it.
- **Verbs, and one idea per sentence.** "Book online. Pay when the job is done." beats "a seamless booking and payment experience".
- **Cutting words never cuts a must-have; it moves it**: proof as a ruled line beside its claim, an objection as a statement with its answer in a disclosure row, prices and terms in a plain table.
- **No empty or unprovable words.** Hype ("best", "world-class") and claims nobody can check ("trusted by thousands") are replaced by the fact behind them, or cut.

## Proof

Proof makes a claim believable. It is asked for in the question message (`ask.md`, question 4): the rating and number of reviews, three to five real reviews word for word with first name and date, years trading and customers or jobs served, accreditations or registrations, guarantees and policies, press, and named staff.

- **Beside the claim it supports.** Proof works where the doubt arises: the review about speed beside the speed claim, the guarantee under the price. A band of testimonials far from any claim does less.
- **On the first screen, one proof line near the action is the default** (the rating and its count, say), when the owner gave proof; on a full-bleed picture opening it may sit just below the fold. Quotes, counts, a second rating and second links go below (`first-screen.md`).
- **Connective detail** (a qualification, a schedule) comes from the owner too, or is a marked stand-in: an invented detail is as false as an invented review.
- **Never write a quote, rating, count or statistic the owner did not give**, for a real business.
- **When a real business has given none**, use proof true by construction (a stated policy, a published price, the product working on a real example) or omit it; never "reviews coming soon". List what to collect under "Before launch".
- **An invented demo business**, all sample data, may carry sample reviews and figures so the design can be judged with them in place, said once in the footer. Never on a page for a real business, even as a placeholder: placeholders ship.

## Objections

Name the top three reasons this visitor would not act, from the answers or inferred from the kind of business: a price surprise, discomfort, time, lock-in, the safety of their money or data, "will it work for me?". Answer each in two places:

- **In the sentence beside the claim**: "[claim], even if [objection]". "A fixed price before we start, even if the job takes longer." "Cancel any time, even in the first month."
- **In a short questions block near the action**: three to six questions in the visitor's words, each answered in one or two plain sentences, open or in plain disclosure rows, beside a side that carries something, or full width (`flow.md`).

## The action

- **One primary action, repeated at the decision points** (the first screen, after the proof, after the price, the end), always in the same words.
- **The label names what the visitor gets**: "Book my service", "Start free". Never "Submit", "Learn more" or "Click here".
- **One short line under every primary button** answers the fear of clicking: "No card needed. Cancel any time.", "Takes two minutes".
- **Take the risk off the visitor** where the owner has agreed to (a guarantee, free cancellation, a fixed quote, a free trial), beside the action and the price, with its condition. State only what the owner agreed.

## Prices

- **Show the total**, every mandatory fee in it. A fee that appears only at checkout is unlawful in several places and costs trust everywhere.
- **A "was" price needs a real earlier price** the business actually charged.
- Where the price depends on the job, show how it is worked out and a real starting price or range, not "Get a quote" alone.

- **Urgency is true and live** ("only 2 left" from the real system) or absent; no pre-ticked extras, no confirm-shaming, no cancellation harder than signing up.

## The order of the argument

This is the one order of the page, used by the section plan (`build.md`). A default, to adapt:

1. The offer and the action.
2. Proof.
3. How it works, or what you get.
4. The main objection, answered.
5. The price.
6. Who is behind it: faces, names, the place.
7. Questions.
8. The action again.

Adapt it to how much the visitor already knows: those comparing get the offer and the price sooner, on a shorter page; those who do not yet see the problem get the problem first, in their words, on a longer page. Echo the wording of where they came from (a search, an ad) in the headline.

This is the order of the visitor's questions, not a template of sections. Several answers can share a section, and the kit's idea carries each one (`build.md`).

## The content pass

Before the reviewer sees a Persuade page, go through this list from the rendered page, and fix what fails in the same round:

- A stranger can say, after five seconds, what it is, for whom or where, and the outcome.
- Every claim has its proof beside it, or is cut.
- Three objections are answered, beside their claims and in the questions block; nothing on this list was cut to save words, only moved into a table or disclosure.
- There is one action, in the same words everywhere, with its reassurance line under it; the owner's proof, if any, is near it.
- Every price is the total, with its condition.
- Nothing is invented for a real business: every quote, rating, count, figure and connective detail (a qualification, a schedule) came from the owner or is a marked stand-in, and what is missing is listed under "Before launch".

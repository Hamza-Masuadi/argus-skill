# simplify

Remove what is not earning its place. A screen gets clearer mostly by subtraction, and a model's instinct is to add, so this command exists to push the other way.

## Find the job

State in one sentence what the user is here to do. Everything on the screen is judged against that sentence.

## Sort every element

Go through the screen and put each element in one bin:

- **Essential**: the user cannot do the job without it. Keep, and make it more prominent.
- **Supporting**: helps some users some of the time. Keep but quieten, or move behind one step (a disclosure, a menu, a detail view).
- **Decorative**: neither. Remove, unless it is the single deliberate piece of character the design allows itself.

Be honest about the third bin. Typical residents: icons that repeat their label, badges and counters nobody acts on, dividers between things already separated by space, boxes around things that are not objects, secondary buttons that duplicate a nearby link, helper text that restates the label, statistics added to fill a row.

## Then reduce

1. **Choices.** One primary action. Cut options few people use, or apply a sensible default instead of asking.
2. **Words.** Halve the copy, then try halving it again. Remove sentences that would fit any product.
3. **Containers.** Take away boxes, borders and backgrounds until grouping still reads from space and alignment alone. Put back only what is needed.
4. **Styles.** Fewer text styles, fewer colours, fewer radii, fewer shadow levels.
5. **Steps.** Remove a screen, a field or a confirmation from the flow if the job still completes.

## Guardrails

- Do not remove what users need to feel safe: prices, consequences, a way back, the status of their work.
- Do not hide primary navigation or frequent actions to look clean.
- Simple is not empty. Product screens can be dense and still simple if everything shown is in the essential bin.
- Removing a feature is a product decision. Propose it; do not do it silently.

## Report

What was removed, what was quietened or moved, and what was kept after consideration with the reason.

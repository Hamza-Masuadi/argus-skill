# Create: new Persuade and Read work

For a new page with nothing to match, where the job is to persuade or to be read. (New dashboards, tools and app screens use `product.md` instead, which borrows step 4's choosing page.) The aim is a page that could only belong to this product: a first screen worth a second look, and every section below it made of the same material.

The route: brief, with a short look at the nearest competitors where that is possible; the subject's kit and the idea built on it; the move, the motion and the section plan; rough directions shown on one choosing page and chosen by looking; a written direction; the first screen built to finished quality and judged against a bar; the rest of the page built from the plan; the loop; and a record of what was built.

**Contents**

1. **Brief**: the site in two or three lines, candidate headlines, an optional look at competitors.
2. **The idea**: the subject's kit, candidate ideas, the two registers, which directions to build.
3. **The move, the motion and the page**: the big move, the live element, the section plan.
4. **Rough directions**: first screens only, the choosing page, the pick.
5. **Write the direction**: its written shape, recorded in `DESIGN.md`.
6. **The first screen**: built to finished quality.
7. **The bar**: six questions the first screen must pass before the rest.
8. **The rest**: every section, then the loop with its section pass.
9. **Record**: complete `DESIGN.md` with what was built.

**What to read, and when.** Read each file at the step that needs it, never before, and once in a session: a later step that needs a file already read uses what you took from it, and re-opens only a named short section when it must check against it (the default looks in `tells.md`, say). Where a section is named, read only that section.

| Step | Read |
|---|---|
| 1 Brief, and the headlines | `tells.md`, skimmed; the roughs and the loop are checked against it later, re-opening only its "Whole default looks" if needed |
| 2 The idea | `structures.md`; `imagery.md`, "The kind of picture" and "The ladder"; `ingredients.md` when choosing which directions to build |
| Photo search (before step 4) | `photos.md`, "Look at every image first", if the decision is photographs or a direction uses the mood photograph (its keep test applies to that one too) |
| 4 Roughs | `craft.md`, now that something is being built; `assets/compare.html` is copied, not read |
| 5 Direction, and `DESIGN.md` | `design-md.md`; use `ingredients.md` from step 2 (read it now only if step 2 was skipped) |
| 6 First screen | `web.md` or `mobile.md`; the rest of `photos.md` if photographs are used, or for the mood photograph its "A mood photograph" section; `imagery.md`, "Drawing in code", only if drawing |
| 8 The rest, and the loop | `verify.md`, `slips.md`, and `review.md` for the reviewer |

**The kind of picture is decided once, before the directions** (`imagery.md`, "The kind of picture"), in one line, from round 1's answers. Where it is photographs and none were supplied, run one batch of searches from the kit before step 4, without asking, and look at the candidates once. The roughs share that batch: no direction gets a search of its own. Where the subject is software or abstract and one direction will use a mood photograph (`imagery.md`), its one or two queries go into the same batch.

## 1. Brief

Before any design choice, write the site in two or three lines (`SKILL.md`, section 3): what it is for, who arrives and from where (the situation they arrive in), and what they do next (the one action). Add two things: why they would choose this over the alternative (the difference), and what they should be sure of after one screen (the belief). Take them from the brief, `DESIGN.md` and the user's answers (`ask.md`). If nobody can be asked, write the assumptions down and continue. Every later choice in this file, the header, the sections, the pictures and the motion, is checked against these lines.

**The headline comes from the difference or the belief**, never from the layout. Write two or three candidate headlines now, short (often under eight words), each saying the difference or the belief in words the visitor would use. Every direction below takes its headline from this list, not from its picture.

**A look at the competitors (optional).** When the session has a web search or browser tool, and the user named or implied competitors or they are easy to find, look at the two or three nearest for a few minutes. First screens only: the headline, the picture and the look. No more than three sites, no reading past the first screen. Then write three lines into the brief:

```
They all say:   the claim every one of them leads with
They all look:  the ground, the picture and the layout they share
This page:      what it will do differently, from the difference in round 1
```

The third line names something this business does or has, not a style. The three lines feed the swap test (steps 4, 7 and 8) and are recorded in `DESIGN.md` under "This page". Nothing is copied from their pages; what they all share is what this page does not lead with. Skip this step without comment when there is no web tool, for a one-line tweak, or inside an existing product.

## 2. The idea

**If the look is already decided, skip to step 5.** That means the user named the look, gave a reference, or supplied brand assets. Write the direction from what they gave. From a reference, take its specifics (palette, type, density, layout idea, tone) and say which you are carrying over; do not copy another brand's identity wholesale. A feeling ("energetic", "calm") is not a decided look: it narrows the candidates below without choosing one.

Otherwise, read `structures.md` and write the subject's kit before listing a single idea.

**The subject's kit.** Pages that feel as if they understand the business are built out of the thing the customer handles or does, not out of a picture of the business or a metaphor borrowed from somewhere nearby. So first write eight to ten concrete things from the activity itself, in the words the people who do it use, across the kinds below. The examples are drawn from several unrelated trades only to show what each kind means. They are illustrations, not ideas to reuse: none belongs on your list unless your subject really is that trade.

- **Objects handled**: a proving basket, a tailor's tape measure, a record sleeve, a boarding card.
- **Instruments and documents**: a paper pattern, a tide table, the sailings board at the terminal, the price chalked on a slate.
- **Gestures and moments**: the scoring cut before a loaf goes in, chalking a hem, flicking through a crate, the ramp coming down.
- **Systems of marking**: chalk marks on cloth, price stickers, crate dividers, deck and lane numbers.
- **Phrases people really say there**: "first batch is out at seven", "arms by your sides", "anything else in the back?", "foot passengers first".

Things from the building, the street, the history or the mood may go on the list, ranked last. For a bakery in a former bank, the baskets, the scoring cut and the slate come first; the vault comes last.

Then list six or seven candidate ideas. **Each is built on one item from the kit, and names it.** Avoid style labels (minimal, brutalist, glassy), which lead back to stock looks.

**Rank the candidates by closeness to the activity.** What the customer handles or does ranks above the place it happens, which ranks above history, atmosphere or a metaphor from another trade. A metaphor from another trade (a share-price ticker for a tailor) needs a reason the kit could not supply; write the reason or drop the candidate.

**The page is the thing.** Prefer ideas where the page is the thing from the kit, or where the visitor does a small version of the real act on the page: flicks through a crate to find the record, reads a measurement off the tape, folds the paper bag shut to place the order. These beat a drawing of the thing set beside ordinary content. A picture of the premises is the weakest form. Where the subject has its own gesture, that gesture is the interaction and the hero's live element (step 3). Legibility and the action still win over cleverness: an idea that makes the page harder to read or to act on is reworked, not shipped.

**An idea is a mechanism, not a layout with a name.** It can be said in one sentence that a visitor would repeat to someone else, and the sentence names something the visitor sees happen or does: "the prices are chalked on a slate, and a loaf is rubbed off when it sells out", "the page is the sailings board, and the next crossing flips over as you scroll". A sentence that only describes arrangement or atmosphere ("a list beside a large image", "dark and cinematic") describes every competitor too. Write the sentence for each candidate, naming its kit item. Where it is only arrangement or mood, find the mechanism or drop the candidate.

Then cut the list:

- Cross out any candidate not built on a kit item, any that is a picture of the premises, and any metaphor from another trade without a written reason.
- Cross out anything under "Tried" in `DESIGN.md`.
- Cross out what the user's answers rule out.
- Ask two questions of each survivor. Would someone who does this job recognise their own kit on the page? If the logo were removed, could you still tell what kind of business this is from the first screen alone? Drop or rework a candidate that fails both.
- Write each survivor's structure fingerprint (`structures.md`). If two share the same first two lines, they are one idea in different clothes. Replace one.

**Two registers.** Every direction is in one of two registers, and the set must show both:

- **Concept-led**: the page is built around a mechanism from the kit, as above: the page is the thing, the visitor does a small version of the act, a device in every section.
- **Plain**: the page is clean and led by real pictures, photograph-led or product-led (`imagery.md`). Its rules: one typeface for everything, in two or three weights, with headline tracking about -2%; one accent colour, used on one button style; a white or near-white ground, which may carry a slight warm or cool tint taken from the subject (a tint, not a cream), or the photograph itself edge to edge; no boxes or borders around content unless they carry meaning; one message per screen: a headline of about six words, eight at most where the swap test (step 7) adds a specific detail, one sentence, one action and, where there is one, one large picture; generous space, and sections about a screen tall. The numbers are in `craft.md`. A plain page is not an empty one: it is long and simple, every screen carrying one thing. The two picture rules fit together: the kind of picture is decided once for the whole set (photographs, the product, or type), and every direction uses that kind; "one large picture" is the plain register's way of using it, one picture of that kind per screen, shown large, each screen a different one from the shared batch, or set type-led where there is none. Its white or near-white ground counts as the set's one pale neutral ground (below), so the concept-led directions beside it take other grounds. Say in the direction which it is.

The plain direction is still made of the subject. What is photographed or shown is the kit: the hands chalking the cloth, the shears, the finished jacket on its stand; the real screen with this business's own data. Its headline comes from the user's answers like any other. And it still needs something alive on the first screen: a looping clip of the real act, a product screen that changes state, or one slow ambient movement (step 3).

**In every set of three or more directions, exactly one is in the plain register**, photograph-led or product-led, so the user always sees it beside the concept-led ones. It may be the wildcard. Where the picture decision is diagrams or type-led (an abstract subject), the plain direction is type-led: the same rules, with type set large as the picture. With one direction only, choose the register from the subject and the user's answers: the pictures the subject takes and what they supplied, the references they admire, and what they said would be wrong for them (`ask.md`). Record the register in the direction (step 5), at the start of its `idea` line in `directions.json` (step 4), and under "Tried" in `DESIGN.md`.

Keep the strongest survivors by the ranking above. Then choose which to build. Keep as many as the number of directions the user asked to see (`ask.md` round 2): three if they did not say, five at most. If they asked for one, keep the strongest, choose its register as below, skip the roughs and the choosing page, and go to step 5.

- **They must really differ.** Each direction uses a different first-screen move (step 3), a different structure fingerprint, and a different colour strategy and main hue family (`ingredients.md`), with display faces of different character (for the plain direction, its one family counts as its display face). Their grounds differ visibly: at most one is a pale neutral (off-white, cream, pale grey or grey-green, white included) and at most one is dark. A second coloured ground is allowed in a clearly different hue. A dark ground needs a reason from how or when the thing is used (step 4). At most one uses a condensed all-capitals display face, and at most one stages its idea as a document or paper object (a sheet, a form, a ticket, a page of a book). The first-screen compositions differ too: never every one with the headline on the left and an object or card on the right, but, for example, one full-bleed picture with the headline on it, one centred or type-only, one split. A handwriting or script face is not a display face for headlines unless the user supplied it. Two directions built around the same central object or interaction are one direction: replace one.
- **No straw men.** Build no direction you would not be willing to pick yourself. These rules make the set differ; they do not open slots to fill. A direction made only to be different (a dark stage with no reason, condensed capitals for contrast) is replaced by the next survivor; if none is left, list more candidates from the kit.
- **The wildcard.** Number the survivors and choose one by chance, not by taste: `echo $((RANDOM % N))`. This keeps the set from being only your preference. Note which one it is now; the tag goes into `directions.json` when that file is written in step 4.
- **The rest**: the survivors ranked closest to the activity that also differ most from the wildcard and from each other.
- **Your pick is not chosen yet.** It is decided in step 4, by looking at the roughs, not before anything is rendered. The wildcard may win: if the direction chosen by chance does best on the questions in step 4, it is both the wildcard and your pick.
- **A new set.** If the user asks for different directions, they can ask for safer or bolder. Build a fresh set from candidates not yet shown (list more if needed), by the same rules, and add the rejected ones to "Tried".

## 3. The move, the motion and the page

Three things every candidate must have before it is worth building. They are defined here because the roughs in step 4 depend on them.

**One big move on the first screen.** Restraint everywhere reads as timid, and a careful page with nothing large on it is forgotten. The move is one of:

- **The photograph**: one excellent image of the kit, very large, often edge to edge, with nothing fighting it (`photos.md`). The default for a real subject, and for the plain register.
- **The product, live**: a working piece of the product, at a size where it shares the stage with the headline, running on a realistic example and starting in an interesting state. The default for software and anything with a screen. One direction in a set may set it in front of or beside a single mood photograph (`imagery.md`).
- **A made picture**: one image built for this page from the subject's own material: a map of the real routes, a chart computed from real figures, a scene. It fills most of the first screen and the headline and action sit on or in it. Get it by the ladder in `imagery.md`; it is drawn in code only where drawing is the right rung, and then as an illustration, not a diagram. Draw the thing the customer handles, not the building.
- **Colour**: the brand colour covers the whole first screen. This is a committed ground (`ingredients.md`): at most two directions in a set use it, and then in clearly different hues.
- **Scale**: one element far larger than a cautious design would allow. Large type on a flat ground is the weakest form of this; give it something to sit in or against.
- **Structure**: the whole page takes the form of something from the kit: the paper pattern, the sailings board, the record crate.

The move suits the business and the belief. For a loud brief, it is scale, a made picture and things that respond. For a calm or premium one, it is space, one perfect image or one exact detail; the move is then a commitment to very little, held just as firmly. The move never costs the action: the primary action stays on the first screen, readable and working, at desktop and phone width. A striking first screen whose form leads nowhere loses to a simple one that works. Carry it out fully: a bold idea at 60% looks like a mistake.

**Something on the first screen lives.** Every new Persuade page has a live element in the hero: one thing drawn from the subject's own gesture that moves or responds from the first second. Something travels along its route, fills, opens, counts, follows the pointer or the scroll: the ramp lowers as the booking form comes into view, the tape measure runs out to the size you type. A still object beside a form is not enough unless the direction's Refuses line says why (a very calm or solemn subject, for example). In the plain register the live element is quiet: a muted looping clip of the real act, a product screen that moves between two real states, or one slow ambient movement in the photograph. The first screen also arrives: it assembles in a short, staggered sequence under about a second. Below it, sections arrive as they enter view, and one or two may be tied to the scroll where the idea calls for it. The layers, their numbers and their safety rules are in `craft.md` (Motion); the safety rules are never traded for effect.

**One idea, all the way down.** A strong first screen above ordinary sections reads as a costume, and this is where most pages fall short.

- **The section plan.** Before building, write one row per section:

  ```
  Section        | Answers                    | Built from (kit) | Visual                         | Device or motion
  The slate      | what is baked today, price | the price slate  | the slate, drawn, chalk grain  | a loaf rubs off when it sells out
  The scoring    | how it is made             | the scoring cut  | a loaf in section, three depths| the cut draws as it scrolls in
  ```

  Each main section is built from a different kit item where the kit allows, so the page uses the kit and not one item of it. The surface, lettering and markings of the idea continue down the page: the same paper, the same chalk, the same numbering.
- **The header comes from this plan.** Its links, if any, are the sections in this plan worth jumping to, in page order and in the plan's words; it adds the one thing this kind of visitor looks for first, and nothing the page does not have (`web.md`, "The header and the footer", read at step 6).
- **Every main section has something to look at**, at a size that matters: a photograph, the product in a new state, an object, a worked example shown large, or, where pictures are few, its type set large (type-led, `imagery.md`). Fewer, bigger pictures beat one small picture per section. A page of headings, paragraphs and cards is a defect on a new Persuade page.
- **Devices.** In the concept-led register, every main section also carries a device: something to look at or do beyond a heading and a paragraph, such as a comparison, a worked example, a control that recalculates, tabs between cases, a drawing that completes as it scrolls. Put the idea's language in the headings and the content themselves, not in small labels stacked above them.
- **The plain register's plan** is one message per section: a short heading, one sentence, at most one action, and one large picture where the page has one for it, or the section set type-led. The device rule relaxes to a different picture, product state or type-led statement in each section; the section plan's last column names it instead of a device.
- **Never force it.** A section may be simple (a heading, a short text, its picture) when no device honestly fits. A device that makes information harder to reach than a list would is a defect: hiding answers behind drawn objects to click, making the visitor play to read a price. Questions and answers are shown as readable text, open on the page or in plain disclosure rows.
- **The section test.** Look at a screenshot of each section alone and ask: could it sit unchanged on a site for a different business? If yes, rebuild it from the kit.
- The page makes the whole argument, in a visitor's order: what it is, how it works, what you get, why believe it, what about my objection, what it costs or what the catch is, what to do now. Several answers can share a section. With no real proof to hand, "why believe it" is the product shown working on a specific example, never invented customers or numbers.
- **Long is fine; dense is not** (`craft.md`). A page may run long when each screen carries one message. Flag it past 14 screens at 390 or 16 at 320, or any section past about 2.5 screens at 390, and fix it by splitting crowded sections or cutting words, never by stripping a section of its picture or its character. `check.js` measures both. Check the length at phone width before the loop ends.
- **One primary action** means one kind of action in one style. It may appear several times: on each bookable item, at the start and at the end. Keep it next to the thing it acts on.
- Say everything once. This applies to content, never to the action.
- Time-based content (sessions, sailings, appointments) opens on what is next and still bookable, never on what has already started or left.

A feeling is delivered by several levers at once: colour, size, pace, density, motion, imagery, wording. Size alone gives a page that is large and lifeless, and colour alone gives a page that is one flat hue. A feeling word is never a colour choice on its own (`ingredients.md`).

**Type that commits.** Each direction has one display face with real character, used at a size that commits (`craft.md`), with its weight and tightness set on purpose, over a quiet body face. Where the subject has its own lettering, take the display face from it: the painted sign over a counter, the stencil on a terminal sign, the type on a record label. A handwriting or script face is not a headline face unless the user supplied it; where the subject's lettering is handwritten, take its character into a printed face, or keep the hand for a single small mark. A condensed all-capitals face is one choice among many, not the default for "bold". The plain register sets everything in one family, in two or three weights, and commits through size, weight and tight tracking instead of a characterful face (`craft.md`). On the first screen the headline is usually the largest thing, or second only to the picture. It is short and says the difference or the belief. If the headline itself is a control (a sentence with a choice inside it), mark it up as `web.md` says.

## 4. Rough directions, chosen by looking

Build the **first screen only** of each chosen candidate, as separate files (`roughs/a.html`, `b.html` and so on): its real headline from step 1, real primary action, its own header in the direction's form (never one stock bar shared by every rough), its real picture, the move at full strength, its live element working, its own palette and display face, rough everything else. No sections below the first screen. The plain rough uses the real photographs from the shared batch or the real product screen, never a grey box standing in for them. Keep them cheap; all but one will be thrown away.

**One quick look per rough**: one capture at 1440 and one at 390 (a browser tool, or `node "<skill>/scripts/shot.js" roughs/a.html --widths 1440,390`), to confirm it renders and nothing is broken. Take it after the arrival has finished: the shot tool waits for it; in a browser pane, finish the animations by script first, with `document.getAnimations().forEach(a => a.finish())`. Broken here means content overflowing or cut off, text overlapping, a picture missing, or text that cannot be read; fix those before the choosing page. Anything else is judged, not fixed, at this stage. No look loop, no capture at four widths, no checker run, no review. The bar (step 7) and the loop apply only to the chosen direction. Then the choosing page.

**The choosing page.** Copy `assets/compare.html` into the same folder as `roughs/index.html`. Beside it, write `roughs/directions.json`, or paste the same list into the page's `const directions = [...]` near the top of its script (that works when the page is opened from a file, where reading a JSON file can fail). One entry per direction, every field filled in for real:

```json
[
  { "file": "a.html", "name": "The Workshop Manual",
    "idea": "Concept-led: the one sentence a visitor would repeat, naming the kit item it is built on",
    "palette": ["#0f3d2e", "#f4f1e8", "#d9472b"],
    "type": "Archivo Black over Source Sans 3",
    "risk": "one line on what could go wrong",
    "tag": "wildcard" }
]
```

Start each `idea` with its register: `Concept-led:` or `Plain, photograph-led:` / `Plain, product-led:` / `Plain, type-led:`. `tag` is `"wildcard"`, `"pick"`, `"wildcard pick"` or `""`. The order: build the roughs and give each its quick look; write `directions.json` with `"wildcard"` on the one chosen by chance in step 2 and every other tag empty; open the choosing page; decide your pick by looking at it there; then add `"pick"` (below) and reload. The palette lists ground, ink and accent, in that order, plus any second accent. The page shows each direction as a card: a live preview at desktop size (with a Desktop/Phone switch; add `#phone` to the address to start at phone size), its tags, name, idea, palette swatches, type and risk, an "Open full size" link and a "Choose this" button that copies a ready sentence for the user to paste into the chat. With no list, it falls back to file names in the address (`index.html?a.html,b.html,c.html`) and then to the folder's listing, with file names as titles; always write the list.

Open the page's **full address**, for example `http://localhost:PORT/roughs/index.html`. Never open the server root or a folder: that shows a file listing, not the directions. Take screenshots after web fonts have loaded (wait for `document.fonts.ready`); a rough captured in a fallback font is judged on the wrong type.

- **Default looks first.** Hold each rough against the whole default looks in `tells.md`, from the step 1 skim (re-open that section if you need it). A rough that shares three features with one look goes back to its idea: rebuild it from its kit item, or replace it with the next survivor. A new colour or face on the same look does not count as a change.
- **Then decide your pick, by looking.** Anything broken was fixed at the quick look: a direction should lose on its idea, not on a bug. With every rough on the choosing page, ask the questions under "Choosing yourself" below. Write `"pick"` on the winner in `directions.json` (or the inline list) and reload the page to confirm the tag shows, before asking the user. If the winner is the wildcard, its tag becomes `"wildcard pick"`.
- **A mix is allowed.** If the strongest result takes its structure from one rough and something else from another, say which came from where and write the direction for the merged version.
- **If the user asked to see directions** (three by default, up to five), screenshot the choosing page itself and confirm every card shows its preview, name, idea, swatches, type and risk; that page is what the user sees. If the environment shows the user a preview pane or link, it is on the choosing page when you ask. The question in chat stays words only: one option per direction, its name and one plain sentence with its idea and its trade-off, plus "mix" in plain words. Never put drawings made of text characters, markup or code in a question or its preview. Do not rank them beyond the `pick` tag unless asked. Wait for the answer; this is the one choice worth waiting for. If nobody is there, choose yourself.
- **Choosing yourself.** Your pick is the direction whose first screen a visitor would understand and trust fastest, and which will hold up for the whole page, not only its first screen. Judge by looking at the roughs, in this order: which says soonest what this is and what to do, with headline, picture and action read in a few seconds at 390 as at 1440; which looks most like a business this visitor would trust with this job, its pictures and words real and nothing reading as a gimmick; which can carry every section of the plan without being forced or repeated; and which could only be this business: put a competitor's name on each first screen (the swap test, step 7). A rough that is clear and trustworthy but would serve a rival unchanged loses to one that is equally clear and trustworthy and specific. Novelty is not a criterion, and neither is cleverness or drama: the most inventive rough wins only when it also wins on these questions. Specific is not the same as novel: a price, a rule or an hour that only this business has is specific however plainly it is set, and a striking layout any rival could use is novel without being specific. Both registers are judged by the same questions, and neither is the safe or the default choice. A weakness a small change would remove (an action placed too low) is not counted against a rough; it is fixed in the winner when it is built. Check the pick does no harm to the subject: nothing printed over the product, nothing cut off.
- **A dark pick needs a reason.** If your pick has a dark ground, write the reason from the subject or from when the thing is used (a night callout, a stargazing night out) into the direction. "It looks more striking" is not a reason. With no reason, pick again or build the winner on a light ground.

If nothing can be rendered, choose by seed, not by preference: `echo $((RANDOM % N))`, or the word count of the brief modulo N. Preference returns the default.

## 5. Write the direction

Use `ingredients.md`, read at step 2, for the token roles every direction fills in and the options for colour, type, shape and space (read it now only if step 2 was skipped). Make each choice for this subject, then write the direction, short, in this shape:

```
Register:    concept-led, or plain (photograph-led, product-led or type-led)
Idea:        the mechanism, in the one sentence a visitor would repeat
Kit:         the items from the subject's kit the page is built on, the hero's first
World:       where the look is drawn from
Structure:   the fingerprint: what leads, section order, heading placement, rhythm
Combination: colour strategy + type characters + shape and space
Palette:     the token roles filled in, with values; each hue named for the thing
             in the subject's world it is taken from
Type:        display and body families, the display size, weight and tracking
Imagery:     the one-line picture decision (imagery.md), where each picture comes
             from (the ladder), which sections are type-led, and the one treatment
Move:        the big move on the first screen
Motion:      the live element; the arrival; reveals; any scroll-linked section;
             the one ambient loop, if any
Header:      its links from the section plan, its one extra item, its form on the
             first screen, and in one line what it does on scroll
Sections:    the section plan from step 3
Refuses:     two or three things this design will not do, starting from what the
             user said would be wrong for them
```

Could the same direction describe a different product in another industry? Then it is a mood, and not yet decided.

Choose light or dark from where and when the thing is used, never from the category or for drama. Let the kit set the ground where it can: a chalk slate, pattern paper, the white of a boarding card. A dark ground carries its reason in the direction (step 4). Build one mode unless asked for both.

Where it goes: `DESIGN.md` (read `design-md.md` now for its shape). "Product" and "This page" are already there, written straight after the question rounds (`ask.md`); create the file now only if the rounds were skipped. Add "Direction" from this step, and under "Tried" every direction shown and not chosen, with its register, fingerprint, colour strategy and main hue. Record the one-line picture decision under "Images", and where each image came from (`imagery.md`). Do not stop to ask. Step 9 completes the same file once with what was built; it is one file, not several documents.

## 6. Build the first screen to finished quality

Keep the winning rough and build the first screen out from it, completely, before anything below it. Write its copy first. Turn the direction into tokens (colour roles, type scale, spacing, radii, motion durations and the two easings) in whatever form the project uses, with the numbers from `craft.md`, and build with tokens only. `web.md` or `mobile.md` says what goes where.

Finish means a designer with another day would have little to add:

- **Depth and light**: layers, a horizon, shading, a gradient that means something (time of day, distance, a lamp), not a flat fill.
- **Detail in the picture**: the small true things from the subject (sailing times, chalk dust, tick marks on a tape, real figures on the product screen). A photograph is cropped and placed so its subject reads at once (`photos.md`); a drawing follows `imagery.md`.
- **The live element and the arrival** working, each with its reduced-motion version (`craft.md`).
- **Type on the picture**: the headline placed with the image, not boxed beside it; sizes and line breaks set by eye.
- **Feedback**: hover, press and focus on every control, as `craft.md` gives them.
- **The header**, built as the direction's Header line says and as `web.md` ("The header and the footer") describes, on phone as on desktop.
- **Nothing lost**: the action on the first screen and working, leading to its minimal target (step 7), and text contrast passing over the picture.
- **The whole width**: at 1920 the first screen still fills the screen, not a narrow column in wide margins (`craft.md`).

## 7. The bar for the first screen

Judge it as a separate look: take fresh screenshots at 390, 1440 and 1920, look at them as if someone else had made it, and answer each question in writing, yes or no:

1. Is it made of the kit, so someone in the trade would recognise their own things?
2. Does something on it live, from the first second?
3. Is there one image, drawing or object at real size, not a small decoration?
4. Is the action obvious, and does it work when used? For this bar the action must go somewhere real: its minimal working target (the form, booking step or destination it leads to, unstyled is fine) is built with the first screen, and finished in step 8.
5. Would you screenshot it and send it to someone?
6. Could a competitor put their name on this first screen unchanged? If yes, it fails. Bring one specific thing from this business's kit or answers onto the first screen, in the headline, the picture or the live element: a rule of theirs, a price, a place, an hour, a way of working, a number only they have. The page stays as clean as it was; this is one specific thing, not decoration. The competitor lines from step 1, where there are any, say what not to repeat.

A "no" to any of the first five, or a "yes" to the sixth, means rework, then judge again from new screenshots. Allow two rounds of rework. If it still fails, go back to the directions (step 4) and take another, once only, saying so in the report; the new one gets the same two rounds. With one direction there is no other to take: then, or when the switched direction also fails, build on with the best version so far and list the questions it still fails in the report under "Open". Otherwise, only when all six pass, build the rest. This bar comes before the loop and is not among its rounds.

## 8. Build the rest, then give it its own pass

Build each section from the section plan, at the same finish as the first screen: its kit item, its picture from the `imagery.md` ladder or its type-led statement, its device or motion (or, in the plain register, its one message), the idea's surface and markings. Write each section's copy before laying it out.

Then run the loop in `SKILL.md`. Its first round is also the section pass: from that round's captures, go through every section at 390 and 1440 and add to the round's list whatever fails here:

- Is it made of its kit item, and does it pass the section test (step 3)?
- Does it have something to look at, at a size that matters (its picture, product state or type-led statement), finished to the first screen's standard?
- Does it have its device (concept-led) or its one message (plain)? Is any device easier to use than a plain list would be? Does its motion follow `craft.md`: arrives as it enters view, nothing hidden without script, a reduced-motion version?
- Does the surface, lettering and marking of the idea continue?
- Is the action, where it appears, the same and working?
- Is every header link a real section in the visitor's words, and is the header's one extra item what this kind of visitor looks for first?

Then the swap test once for the whole page: put a competitor's logo on it and count the sections that would survive unchanged. Each one that would gets one specific thing from this business (its rule, price, place, hours, way of working, a number only it has), in its heading, its picture or its device; not decoration, and not a section made busier.

In the same round, check against `tells.md` from your step 1 reading (re-opening "Whole default looks" if you need it), the whole default looks included: a page that shares three features with one goes back to its idea, not to a different coat of paint; the checker measures the length at 390 and 320 and each section's height.

A direction drawn from the subject may share a trait with a tell (a boarding card sets its figures in mono). That exception covers only the trait the chosen object itself has, only where that object appears. Name each one used in the report.

## 9. Record what was built

The last step, after the loop. Complete `DESIGN.md` with what was actually built, so the next session can extend the design from the file alone: the final palette tokens with their values, type families and sizes, spacing and radii, the components that exist and where they live, the motion layers and their tokens, where each image came from and under what licence, and any decision that changed during the build, with its reason. The direction written in step 5 was the plan; this is the record. Say in the report that it was written or updated.
